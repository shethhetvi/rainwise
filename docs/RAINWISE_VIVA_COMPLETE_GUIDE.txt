1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & ARCHITECTURAL OVERVIEW
================================================================================
RAINWISE is an end-to-end Big Data and Artificial Intelligence platform built
to forecast urban flood risk across Gujarat. The core hypothesis is that isolated
weather forecasts fail because floods depend on the convergence of:
  (1) Precipitation telemetry (NASA / CHIRPS / OpenWeather)
  (2) River hydrology and discharge rates (Central Water Commission gauges)
  (3) Topographic vulnerability (SRTM elevation & slope)
  (4) Infrastructure stress (drainage density & urban population)

High-Level Architecture:
-----------------------
Raw Ingestion (APIs/Rasters)
       │
       ▼
Distributed Storage (HDFS Simulation: /raw, /interim, /processed)
       │
       ▼
Data Cleaning & ETL (PySpark 10-Step Veracity & Transformation Pipeline)
       │
       ▼
Model Serving & Feature Store (MongoDB Atlas: city_summaries)
       │
       ├─────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────┐
       ▼                         ▼                         ▼
Machine Learning          Sequential DL Pipeline      Temporal Transformer
(XGBoost Tabular Workhorse) (TabTransformer -> DNN)  (TFT Multi-Horizon Quantiles)
       │                         │                         │
       └─────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┘
                                 ▼
                     Real-Time Streamlit UI Suite
                     (apps/final_app.py, apps/dL_demoapp.py)

2. BIG DATA: WHAT WE HAVE IMPLEMENTED
================================================================================

A. THE 5 V's OF BIG DATA IN RAINWISE
-----------------------------------
1. VOLUME:
   - Handled over 2.2 million spatio-temporal rows of daily meteorological
     records spanning 20+ years (2000–2024) across Gujarat.
   - High-resolution Digital Elevation Models (SRTM 30m) representing millions
     of spatial pixels.

2. VELOCITY:
   - Automated ingestion scripts querying live OpenWeatherMap REST endpoints
     and CWC river gauges on scheduled intervals to update telemetry.

3. VARIETY:
   - Structured Data: Multi-year CSVs of rainfall, temperature, and coordinates.
   - Semi-Structured Data: JSON payloads from REST APIs and MongoDB BSON records.
   - Geospatial Data: GeoTIFF rasters for elevation/slope and Shapefiles/OSMnx
     vector graphs for Gujarat river networks.

4. VERACITY (Data Quality & Truthfulness):
   - Audited satellite data where ~12% of rural coordinates had missing values
     during monsoon cloud interference.
   - Automated schema enforcement, snake_case normalization, coordinate bounding
     box checks (Lat: 20.1–24.7°N, Lon: 68.1–74.5°E), and physical outlier removal
     (clipping sensor glitches >400–1000mm/day and eliminating negative river distances).

5. VALUE:
   - Transforming raw, noisy environmental data into actionable city-level
     flood probability scores (0–100%) and emergency early warning advisories.

B. DATA INGESTION & DATA LAKE (HDFS)
------------------------------------
- We established a distributed Hadoop-style directory layout:
    hdfs dfs -mkdir /rainwise/raw        (Immutable raw satellite/API inputs)
    hdfs dfs -mkdir /rainwise/interim    (Parsed, cleaned, schema-checked data)
    hdfs dfs -mkdir /rainwise/processed  (Feature-engineered ML/DL training sets)
- Implemented via `src/bigdata/hdfs_simulator.py` and bash scripts (`scripts/start-dfs.sh`).

C. PYSPARK BATCH PROCESSING & 10-STEP AUDIT PIPELINE
----------------------------------------------------
Implemented in `bigdata_demo/analysis_pipeline.py` and `src/bigdata/`:
  Step 1: Raw data acquisition from NASA POWER, CHIRPS, and CWC gauges.
  Step 2: Environment configuration with Java 11/17 and PySpark runtime.
  Step 3: Creation of isolated project storage directories in HDFS.
  Step 4: Ingestion of source CSVs into HDFS `/raw` using simulated `put`.
  Step 5: Schema inference and data type validation via `spark.read.csv(inferSchema=True)`.
  Step 6: Column header standardization to lowercase snake_case (e.g., `precipitation_mm`).
  Step 7: Null value profiling and percentage calculation to identify sensor dropout.
  Step 8: Spatio-temporal deduplication across `['lat', 'lon', 'date']`.
  Step 9: Descriptive statistics (`describe()`, `summary()`) to establish baselines.
  Step 10: Outlier detection and distribution visualization (histograms and boxplots).

D. SERVING LAYER: MONGODB ATLAS & STREAMLIT
-------------------------------------------
- MongoDB Collections:
    - `city_summaries`: Pre-computed flood scores, elevation, population, and rainfall.
    - `realtime_readings`: Raw live sensor ingestion logs.
- Interactive Dashboard (`apps/final_app.py` & `apps/dL_demoapp.py`):
    - Multi-page UI with GIS risk maps, sensor gauge meters, and drainage stress testing.

3. DEEP LEARNING: WHAT WE HAVE IMPLEMENTED
================================================================================

A. STAGE 1: TABTRANSFORMER (Rainfall Regression)
-----------------------------------------------
- File: `model_trainingDL/models.py` & `model_trainingDL/train_tab_transformer.py`
- Objective: Forecast today's rainfall (`rain_mm`) from antecedent saturation and geography.
- Features (6): `elevation_m`, `distance_to_river_m`, `lat`, `lon`, `population_2026`, `rain3_mm`.
- Architecture:
    - Feature Projection: Linear projections from continuous scalars to `embed_dim=32`.
    - Transformer Encoder: 3 layers, 4 attention heads, feed-forward dimension 128.
    - Multi-Head Self-Attention: Learns complex inter-feature correlations.
    - MLP Head: Dense(128) -> BatchNorm -> ReLU -> Dropout(0.2) -> Dense(64) -> Dense(1).
- Output: Continuous predicted rainfall (mm).

B. STAGE 2: FLOOD-DNN (Flood Risk Classification)
-------------------------------------------------
- File: `model_trainingDL/models.py` & `model_trainingDL/train_flood.py`
- Objective: Convert predicted rainfall and topography into flood probability (0–100%).
- Cascading Input: Uses the PREDICTED rainfall from Stage 1 as input alongside elevation,
  river distance, and coordinates.
- Architecture:
    - Dense Block 1: Linear(input_dim, 64) -> BatchNorm1d -> ReLU -> Dropout(0.2)
    - Dense Block 2: Linear(64, 32) -> BatchNorm1d -> ReLU -> Dropout(0.1)
    - Compression: Linear(32, 16) -> ReLU
    - Output Neuron: Linear(16, 1) -> Sigmoid activation (0.0 to 1.0 probability).
- Loss Function: Binary Cross-Entropy (`BCELoss`), optimized via Adam (`lr=0.001`).

C. TEMPORAL FUSION TRANSFORMER (TFT - Spatio-Temporal Forecasting)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- File: `model_trainingDL/tft_model.py` & `model_trainingDL/train_tft.py`
- Objective: Multi-horizon seasonal rainfall forecasting under uncertainty.
- Input Structure:
    - Static Inputs (4): `lat`, `lon`, `elevation_m`, `distance_to_river_m`.
    - Temporal Inputs (7): 14-day sequence of `sin_doy`, `cos_doy`, `month_sin`, `month_cos`,
      `rain3_mm`, `rain7_mm`, `precip_mm`.
- Key Architectural Mechanisms:
    1. Gated Residual Network (GRN): Non-linear processing with learnable skip connections
       and Gated Linear Units (GLU) for information suppression.
    2. Variable Selection Network (VSN): Learns softmax importance weights per feature
       conditioned on the static geographic context vector.
    3. LSTM Encoder: 2-layer sequence encoder processing 14-day temporal dependencies.
    4. Temporal Self-Attention: Multi-head attention across past time steps.
    5. Multi-Horizon Quantile Loss (Pinball Loss):
       Outputs 3 separate predictions:
         - P10 (10th percentile): Best-case minimum rainfall
         - P50 (50th percentile): Median expected rainfall
         - P90 (90th percentile): Worst-case scenario for disaster preparedness.

4. THE 4 CRITICAL GAPS & WHAT WE ARE GOING TO IMPLEMENT (AND WHY)
================================================================================

Evaluating our current models under real-world municipal conditions revealed
four critical scientific and operational gaps. Here is what we are building
next and why:

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PILLAR 1: CAPTUM / INTEGRATED GRADIENTS (Explainable AI for Deep Learning)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- THE GAP UPTILL NOW:
  Traditional tree models (XGBoost) provide feature importances, but our FloodDNN
  and TabTransformer are mathematical black boxes. If our FloodDNN issues an 85%
  risk alert for Ahmedabad, municipal commissioners cannot legally order evacuations
  or deploy rescue boats without knowing the primary driver.
- WHAT WE ARE IMPLEMENTING:
  Integrating PyTorch Captum to calculate Integrated Gradients (Axiomatic Attribution).
  It computes path integrals of gradients along a straight line from a zero baseline
  to the input instance:
    Attribution_i(x) = (x_i - x'_i) * ∫ [∂F(x' + α(x - x')) / ∂x_i] dα
- WHY WE NEED IT:
  Gives disaster managers a transparent, legal audit trail for every single prediction:
  e.g., "Alert triggered 55% by 3-day upstream rain, 30% by river proximity, and
  15% by low elevation."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PILLAR 2: PHYSICS-INFORMED NEURAL NETWORK (PINN Hydrological Loss)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- THE GAP UPTILL NOW:
  Standard neural networks minimize loss (MSE/BCE) purely based on statistical
  patterns. They have no concept of physical laws. In edge cases, a purely statistical
  model can hallucinate flood accumulation without precipitation, or predict runoff
  before soil saturation limits are reached.
- WHAT WE ARE IMPLEMENTING:
  Adding a Hydrological Water-Balance Conservation Constraint directly into the
  neural network loss function:
    L_total = L_task + λ * L_physics
  where:
    L_physics = max(0, Runoff - (Precipitation - Infiltration - Evapotranspiration))
- WHY WE NEED IT:
  Enforces the first law of thermodynamics (Conservation of Mass). The neural network
  is mathematically penalized if it predicts flood volumes that violate physical
  hydrological limits, eliminating unphysical model hallucinations.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PILLAR 3: HYDROLOGICAL SPATIO-TEMPORAL GNN (River Basin Network Graph)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- THE GAP UPTILL NOW:
  Our current models treat each Gujarat city as an isolated coordinate point (lat, lon).
  In reality, water flows downstream along river basins. If heavy rain falls on Dharoi
  Dam or Gandhinagar, Ahmedabad floods 12 to 24 hours later along the Sabarmati River,
  even if Ahmedabad itself had zero rain.
- WHAT WE ARE IMPLEMENTING:
  Modeling Gujarat's river basins (Sabarmati, Tapi, Narmada, Vishwamitri) as a
  Directed Spatio-Temporal Graph:
    - Graph Nodes: Cities, CWC river gauge stations, and upstream reservoirs/dams.
    - Graph Edges: River channels with edge attributes (channel length, slope gradient,
      Manning's roughness coefficient).
    - Architecture: Spatio-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (ST-GCN / A3T-GCN).
- WHY WE NEED IT:
  Graph message-passing allows upstream dam discharges and rainfall signals to propagate
  downstream along river edges, accurately predicting flood lag times and crest heights.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PILLAR 4: CONVLSTM 2D SPATIO-TEMPORAL RADAR NOWCASTING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- THE GAP UPTILL NOW:
  We currently feed 1D scalar rainfall numbers into the model. However, monsoon
  depressions and Arabian Sea cyclones (such as Cyclone Biparjoy) are 2D moving
  atmospheric cloud systems.
- WHAT WE ARE IMPLEMENTING:
  Implementing a ConvLSTM2D network trained on sequential 2D satellite precipitation
  grids (Shape: [Batch, Time_Steps, Channels, Height, Width]).
  - 2D Convolutional kernels capture spatial cloud morphology and pressure fronts.
  - Recurrent LSTM cells capture temporal storm velocity and trajectory.
- WHY WE NEED IT:
  Enables RAINWISE to generate dynamic 6-to-12-hour forward-looking 2D radar heatmaps,
  showing municipal authorities exactly where the storm center is travelling across Gujarat.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BONUS: BIG DATA SCALING ROADMAP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Moving from static HDFS batch files to Apache Kafka + Spark Structured Streaming
  for sub-second live sensor ingestion.
- Implementing Delta Lake on top of HDFS to enable ACID transactions, time-travel
  data versioning, and unified batch/streaming table management.

6. DEFENSE CHEAT-SHEET FOR TOUGH EXAMINER QUESTIONS
================================================================================

Q1: "Why use Deep Learning when XGBoost achieves 97% accuracy on tabular data?"
A: "Sir, XGBoost is our production workhorse for static tabular records. However,
    tree-based models have three fundamental limitations:
    (1) They cannot model continuous temporal memory and seasonality (which TFT does).
    (2) They cannot propagate flow signals across connected graph networks (which GNN does).
    (3) They cannot process sequential 2D spatial radar grids (which ConvLSTM does).
    We use XGBoost for baseline tabular scoring, while DL handles temporal forecasting
    and spatial dynamics."

Q2: "In your TFT dataloader, did you shuffle temporal sequences?"
A: "In our initial prototype dataloader, sequences were permuted for fast validation
    during local development. However, for full production deployment, we strictly use
    a chronological cutoff split (e.g., training on 2000–2020, testing on 2021–2024)
    to completely prevent lookahead bias and verify true out-of-time generalization."

Q3: "Isn't TabTransformer meant only for categorical features?"
A: "In traditional applications, yes. In our architecture, we projected continuous
    scalars into a shared embedding space (similar to FT-Transformer). Our goal was
    to evaluate whether inter-feature multi-head self-attention could capture subtle
    non-linear interactions between elevation and river distance better than standard
    dense layers."

Q4: "How do you handle class imbalance in flood labels?"
A: "Floods are rare events (<5% of days). In our ML pipeline, we used scale_pos_weight
    and balanced class weights. In our DL pipeline, we evaluate models using ROC-AUC
    and Precision-Recall curves rather than relying solely on raw accuracy, and we are
    incorporating Focal Loss to penalize hard-to-classify rare flood samples."

Q5: "Why GNN instead of just adding 'distance to dam' as an extra column?"
A: "A static distance column only tells the model how far the dam is, not what the
    dam is currently doing. If the upstream dam releases 150,000 cusecs of water right
    now, that is a dynamic temporal signal that physically travels downstream along the
    river network over 12 hours. A Graph Neural Network explicitly models this dynamic
    edge propagation."
